How much should I charge to remove a tree?

Only you can set that number: it depends on your costs, your region, the access and the hazards on each job. What this page shows is one common way to turn a tree's size into a price: price = starting fee + estimated volume × your rate per cubic foot (or cubic meter). You choose the rate and the fee; this calculator does not use or quote market prices.

What is the formula for the volume of a tree?

The trunk is treated as a cylinder, so the volume is the circumference squared times the height, divided by 12.56:

volume = circumference × circumference × height ÷ 12.56

12.56 is 4π (about 12.566). Because circumference = 2π × radius, the formula equals π × radius² × height, the volume of a cylinder. In feet the answer is cubic feet; in meters it is cubic meters.

Where do I measure the circumference and height?

Wrap a tape around the trunk about 4½ feet (1.4 m) above the ground and read the circumference. Measure height from the ground to the top of the tree; if you cannot reach it with a tape, use the tree height calculator. Use the same unit for both.

What rate per cubic foot should I use?

There is no single right rate. Work backwards from your own numbers: crew and equipment cost per hour, how long a typical tree of that size takes, disposal, insurance and the margin you need. Different jobs (open lawn versus a tight yard, near power lines, crane needed) justify different rates, which is why the app lets you change the rate on each estimate and add separate line items for the extra work.

What does this calculator leave out?

  • It is an approximation for pricing, not a measure of lumber, firewood or biomass. A real trunk tapers and branches add volume.
  • It does not know about access, hazards, utility lines, permits, disposal or cleanup. Price those as extra charges.
  • Tax is optional and is simply your percentage applied to the price. Check your local rules.
  • Results are estimates. You are responsible for checking every figure before quoting it to a customer.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate the price to remove a tree?

Multiply the tree's volume by your rate and add a starting fee. Volume = circumference × circumference × height ÷ 12.56, so a 6 ft circumference, 50 ft tree is 143.3 cu ft; at $10 per cu ft plus a $150 starting fee that is $1,583.12 (example numbers).

Does this calculator tell me what tree removal costs in my area?

No. Rates vary by region, company and job, so you enter your own rate. The numbers on this page are examples.

Can I use meters?

Yes. Choose Meters and enter circumference and height in meters. The volume is in cubic meters and the rate is per cubic meter.
